One absurd clause buried deep in a terms-of-service agreement: **By accepting these Terms, you waive the right to your own reflection, which the Company may use in mirrors, puddles, and haunted houses.**

It reads like real boilerplate — "By accepting these Terms, you waive the
right to…" — until the thing being waived turns out to be your own
reflection, and the list of places it may be used escalates from the
mundane (mirrors) to the odd (puddles) to the outright haunted (haunted
houses). The joke lands on one reading, needs no setup, and comes from the
legal register as much as from the words.

## Run it

```sh
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=/tmp/out python3 main.py
cat /tmp/out/entry.txt
```

The program writes `By accepting these Terms, you waive the right to your own reflection, which the Company may use in mirrors, puddles, and haunted houses.` (136 bytes, one sentence, no trailing newline) to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`.
